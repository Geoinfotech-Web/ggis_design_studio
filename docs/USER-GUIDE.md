# User guide — making a map with GIS Design Studio

A map is made in five steps, shown across the top of the studio:
**Study area → Start map → Data → Compose → Export.** A step that is not open
yet shows a padlock and says what it is waiting for.

## 1. Sign in

Open the studio and choose **Sign up** (new) or **Sign in**. Use your email
and a password (at least 8 characters), or **Sign in with Google**. Your maps
are saved to your account and open on any computer you sign in on.

## 2. Choose the study area

The study area is the place the map is about. Every data layer is cut to it.

- **Anywhere in the world:** set *Country* to **Anywhere in the world** and
  start typing. Pick from the suggestions — countries, states/regions,
  districts/LGAs, towns and named places. Type *"Ikeja, Lagos"* to narrow by
  region or country. Use ↑ ↓ and Enter, or click.
- **A specific country:** pick the country, then a *Level*:
  - *Country* — the whole country.
  - *State / Province* — type or pick the state.
  - *LGA / District* — pick the state or region first, then choose from the
    list of its LGAs/districts.
  - *City / Town* or *Search any place* — type the name.
  - *Geopolitical zone* (Nigeria) — the six zones.
- **Your own boundary:** switch to **Upload my own** and drop a zipped
  Shapefile, GeoJSON, KML, KMZ or GPX file. Shape problems such as
  self-crossing lines are repaired automatically.

Press **Load boundary**. To map several places together, press **Add this
area** for each one; **Replace instead** swaps them all for the new one.
Then press **Continue to Start map**.

## 3. Start the map

Choose one:
- **New map** — a blank page; pick the paper size and orientation.
- **Template** — a finished design (cartographic, report, vintage, 3-D…).
- **Contributor** — a layout used on a real published map sheet.

Your study area is kept whichever you choose, and you can change the
starting point later.

## 4. Add data

Open **Data** and tick what you want — roads, rivers, health facilities,
schools, markets, power lines and 30 more. Layers arrive already coloured
and symbolised by type, and cut to your study area. Most load in under a
second.

- For a **whole country**, very detailed layers (local roads, buildings) are
  too much to draw; the studio offers a lighter layer (e.g. *Major roads*) or
  asks you to pick a smaller area.
- **Upload your own data** (GeoJSON, KML, KMZ, GPX, CSV with coordinates,
  zipped Shapefile) from the Data panel. Files are read in your browser.

## 5. Compose the page

- **Elements** — add a title, subtitle, legend, scale bar, north arrow,
  locator inset, key figures, credits, logo, text and shapes. Drag them on
  the page; guides help you line them up. Select two or more to align or
  space them evenly.
- **Layers** — change colours, symbols, line patterns and line thickness
  (in millimetres, as it will print), colour by category or by value, show,
  hide or reorder layers.
- **Legend** — select the legend on the page to rename rows or change their
  colour, symbol or pattern; the map changes with it.
- **Map style, basemap and page** (from *Start map*) — 19 basemaps including
  satellite imagery, paper size (A6 to A0 and more), orientation, frame.
- Undo / redo: **Ctrl+Z** / **Ctrl+Shift+Z**. All shortcuts: press **?**.

## 6. Export

Press **Export PDF** (or **PNG**) in the header. In page setup choose the
resolution: 96 dpi for screens, 300 dpi for printing, up to 600 dpi for fine
prints. The map is redrawn at that resolution, so it stays sharp. The data
credits required by the data providers are printed on the map automatically.

## Your projects

Your work saves automatically once the map has a study area or data, and you
can save at any time with **Ctrl+S**. **My projects** lists every map, newest
first — open, rename or delete them there. Up to 200 maps per account.

## AI assistant

The **AI assistant** panel writes a plain-language summary of your map for a
report. If your organisation has set up the assistant service, you can also
ask it questions about the map. It only sees the description of the map
(place, layer names, counts) — never your data files.

## Getting help

If something does not load, check your internet connection: basemaps and
some data come from online services. Report problems to the GIS team with
the study area, the layer and a screenshot.
